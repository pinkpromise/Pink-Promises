Start with the basics

Awareness is more than a pink ribbon.

Breast cancer happens when cells in the breast grow abnormally. Knowing the basics can help you make informed choices and seek care when something changes.

♡

Know your body

Be familiar with how your breasts usually look and feel. There is no single “normal” that applies to everyone.

⌕

Notice changes

A new lump is one possible sign, but changes in skin, nipple, shape, or size can matter too.

✚

Get advice

Many breast changes are not cancer. A healthcare professional can assess a change and advise on next steps.

Trust what you notice.

Contact a healthcare professional if you notice a new or unusual change that persists or concerns you. Do not wait for pain: breast cancer can be painless.

Screening is personal.

Age, family history, genetic risk, and local guidance can affect when and how screening is recommended. Ask a qualified healthcare professional what is right for you.

Helpful answers

Questions people often ask.

No. Many lumps are caused by non-cancerous conditions, but a new lump or unusual change should be assessed by a healthcare professional.
Yes. Breast cancer may not cause pain, especially early on. Do not wait for pain before seeking advice about a new change.
Screening advice varies by age, personal and family history, risk factors, and national recommendations. Ask a healthcare professional about the schedule that fits your circumstances.
Tell your healthcare professional about your family history, including which relatives were affected and their age at diagnosis if known. They can assess whether additional guidance or genetic counselling may be useful.
